Alzheimer's Clinical Trials Data Pipeline

A full ETL/ELT pipeline over ~4,200 Alzheimer's disease trials from ClinicalTrials.gov: Python extraction → BigQuery → a tested, documented dbt model (staging → intermediate → marts) → an analytical SQL layer built on window functions and multi-step CTEs.

Key finding: industry-sponsored trials show a clear "Phase 3 valley of death" — termination risk climbs from 11.5% at Phase 1 to 35.3% at Phase 3, the field's most expensive and definitive stage. Over the same period, the share of trials that are classic phase-classified drug trials (rather than observational studies) has fallen from ~65% to ~30%, a plausible structural response to that failure pattern.

Recovering Rare Cell Populations at Scale

An independently built PySpark pipeline — QC, normalization, HVG selection, PCA, clustering — tested against a published single-cell RNA-seq dataset (Di Bella et al. 2021, Nature) with real ground truth: does the pipeline correctly recover a known rare population, Cajal-Retzius cells, and how does recovery reliability change with sample size?

Key finding: the full ~89,000-cell dataset recovers Cajal-Retzius cells at 93.3% recall and 55.2% precision — but a sampling experiment shows recall alone is a misleading signal at smaller scale: at 2,000–10,000 cells, recall stays deceptively high (65–78%) while precision collapses to ~1.4%, meaning the pipeline looks like it's finding the rare population when it's really just returning broad, heterogeneous clusters.

Predicting Churn, From Signal to Retention Decision

A churn model built with methodological rigor as the actual point: leakage-safe preprocessing, a decision threshold locked via cross-validation on training data only (never chosen against the test set), a calibration check, and subgroup error analysis — then a retention-targeting simulation with an explicit sensitivity analysis over the business assumptions, visualized in Power BI.

Key finding: at base-case assumptions, model-based targeting (the locked threshold) beats contacting every customer — but only above a break-even retention success rate of ~39%. Below that, the naive "contact everyone" strategy actually wins. The honest answer isn't "the model works," it's "the model works under these specific, checkable conditions."
